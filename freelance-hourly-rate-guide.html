How to Calculate Your Freelance Hourly Rate

Setting a freelance rate can feel difficult, especially when you are starting out. Charge too little and you may struggle to cover your expenses. Charge too much without explaining the value you provide and you may find it harder to win clients.

A practical starting point is to calculate the income you need, add your business costs, and estimate how many hours you can realistically bill clients each month.

1. Set your monthly income goal

Decide how much you want to earn each month before personal taxes and other deductions. Be clear about whether your target represents business revenue or the money you want to keep personally. These are not the same.

2. Add your business expenses

List the costs of running your freelance business. These may include software subscriptions, internet access, equipment, payment processing fees, professional services, and marketing.

Include a reasonable allowance for taxes and other obligations based on the rules where you live. If you are unsure, consult a qualified tax professional.

3. Estimate your billable hours

Not every working hour can be charged to a client. You will also spend time finding clients, writing proposals, managing invoices, learning new skills, and handling administration.

For example, if you work 30 hours per week, you might initially estimate that 20 to 25 hours are billable. Your actual figure will depend on your workload and business model.

4. Use a simple hourly-rate formula

Estimated hourly rate = (monthly income goal + monthly business expenses + tax allowance) ÷ monthly billable hours

For example, suppose your monthly income goal is $3,000, business expenses are $300, your tax allowance is $700, and you expect 100 billable hours per month.

Your estimated rate would be ($3,000 + $300 + $700) ÷ 100 = $40 per hour.

This is an illustrative calculation, not a guarantee of income or a recommendation for every freelancer.

5. Account for holidays and slow periods

You may not have the same number of billable hours every month. Allow for holidays, illness, client delays, and periods when work is harder to find. A rate based on every available working hour may be too low if only some of those hours generate revenue.

6. Check your rate against the market

Research freelancers with similar skills, experience, and clients. Compare the scope of work and the value delivered, not just the advertised hourly price. Rates can vary substantially by specialty, location, experience, and industry.

7. Review your rate regularly

Track your actual billable hours, expenses, and project results. Review your rate when your skills improve, demand changes, or your costs increase. For projects with uncertain scope, consider whether a fixed project price would be more suitable than hourly billing.

Quick checklist

  • Define your monthly income goal.
  • List business expenses and relevant tax obligations.
  • Estimate realistic monthly billable hours.
  • Calculate a starting hourly rate.
  • Compare your rate with similar services in your market.
  • Review your numbers as your business changes.

Final thoughts

A sustainable freelance rate should cover your business costs, support your income goals, and reflect the work you deliver. Start with a realistic calculation, test it against actual projects, and adjust as you learn more about your business.
